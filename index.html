Why this exists.

From Little Onward exists to make high-quality, science-backed parent education accessible to any parent who seeks it, not only those who can enroll in a graduate program or pay for a specialist.

The reason reaches past any one hard afternoon. Well-adjusted, happy adults start as children whose parents understood what they were building. We teach those parents today.

Why is leaving the playground so hard?

The Developmental Lens · 18 months to 4 years

One ordinary visit in four frames, and what may be developing beneath each. Nothing about the playground changes. The plan does.

1 of 4 · Absorbed in play

Ten minutes on the slide, and everything is fine. You give the two-minute warning.

Beneath the surface

You are inside the system too. Being late, being watched by other parents, being tired: your own state shapes how you respond. Supported. Waters and colleagues, 2014.

What helps

Say what is next before the end. “Two more slides, then lunch.” A plan said out loud gives your child something to hold on to, even if it does not stop the protest.

“Time to go.” Nothing about the playground has changed. The plan has.

Beneath the surface

Switching rules is hard at this age. Most three-year-olds keep sorting by the first rule after being told the new one. Leaving asks for a similar switch, from “play” to “go”, in a moment that matters far more to a child. Well established in laboratory tasks. Zelazo, 2006.

What helps

One small choice inside a firm limit. “Slide or swing for the last one?” Your child gets a say in how you leave, not whether.

Your two-and-a-half-year-old turns and runs back to the slide.

Beneath the surface

The brakes are the last part to arrive. Holding back or stopping an action relies partly on brain systems that grow fastest in the preschool years and keep developing long after. Well established. Diamond, 2013.

Autonomy is the work of this age. “No” is part of becoming a person with a will of one’s own, and it often shows up right when an adult’s plan takes over. Supported. Brownell and colleagues, 2007; Erikson, 1950.

What helps

The hand held low. Close, steady, not grabbing. Narrate the next step as you go: the working memory that holds “home, lunch, nap” is yours for now.

You reach for a hand, and they drop to the ground. Loud, fast crying.

Beneath the surface

Once overwhelmed, reasoning does not land. Words and reasons tend not to land until a child has calmed down. Young children borrow a calm adult to get there. Supported. Diamond, 2013; From Little Onward toddler class, Module 4.

What helps

Regulate first, teach later. Fewer words, a lower voice, a steady body, staying close. The explanation can wait for the car.

Expect it to be hard sometimes anyway. A smooth exit on Tuesday and a floor exit on Wednesday is ordinary at this age, not proof the method failed.

The person behind the classes.

Matthew McArthur holds an M.A. in Developmental Psychology from San Diego State University, where he studied how the home shapes early development, after working with developmental researchers at UCLA. He wrote about early language for children in Frontiers for Young Minds, then spent two years in state-funded early intervention, coaching more than 100 families of children under three.

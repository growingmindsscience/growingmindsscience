Something hard right now? Start here.

Why is leaving the playground so hard?

Four ordinary moments, and what is still growing underneath each one.

Ten happy minutes on the slide.

Then the two-minute warning. Saying what comes next, “two more slides, then lunch,” gives your child something to hold on to.

“Time to go.”

Switching from “play” to “go” is genuinely hard at this age. The skill of changing plans is still being built.

A run back to the slide.

Stopping something fun is one of the last skills to arrive. The brakes are still under construction. It is not a child ignoring you.

Down on the ground, crying.

Once a child is overwhelmed, reasons don’t land. Stay close and calm first. The explaining can wait for the car.

Easy on Tuesday, hard on Wednesday? That is ordinary at this age.

Why this exists.

From Little Onward exists to make high-quality, science-backed parent education accessible to any parent who seeks it, not only those who can enroll in a graduate program or pay for a specialist.

How we teach

The first five years, understood.

One class for each window of the first five years. Each one covers your child, yourself and your influence, because a child’s calm is borrowed before it is owned. You learn the mechanism, the evidence, and the limits of what anyone can know about one child, at your own pace. Ages are typical ranges, not deadlines.

The person behind the classes.

Matthew McArthur holds an M.A. in Developmental Psychology from San Diego State University, where he studied how the home shapes early development, after working with developmental researchers at UCLA. With Dr. Margaret Friend, he wrote “Why Early Chatter Matters” for Frontiers for Young Minds, a journal reviewed by young readers. He then spent two years in state-funded early intervention, coaching more than 100 families of children under three.

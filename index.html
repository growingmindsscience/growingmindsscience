Leaving the playground · 18 months to 4 years

Why “time to go” ends in tears

A minute ago, they were fine.

You call them to leave. They run back to the slide and drop to the ground, crying.

Stopping in the middle of fun is hard.

The urge to keep playing is strong from the start. The ability to stop themselves grows slowly, through the preschool years and beyond.

Before it’s time to leave, say what comes next.

“Two more slides, then home for lunch.”

There may still be tears, but leaving won’t come as a surprise.

Notice what set off the tears.

Was it the warning, the walk to the gate, or the car seat? Each time, you learn a little more about your child.

Why this exists.

Across more than 100 families in early intervention, one pattern held: the parents who did best were, almost without exception, the ones who understood the science.

Children build the adults they become one ordinary day at a time, and understanding that work makes it lighter to carry.

The person behind the classes.

Matthew McArthur holds an M.A. in Developmental Psychology from San Diego State University, where he studied how the home shapes early development, after working with developmental researchers at UCLA. With Dr. Margaret Friend, he wrote “Why Early Chatter Matters” for Frontiers for Young Minds, a journal reviewed by young readers. He then spent two years in state-funded early intervention, coaching more than 100 families of children under three.

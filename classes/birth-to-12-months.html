Class 1 · Birth to 12 months

Birth to 12 months: cues, attachment, and the first year.

A calm, research-based picture of what infants are actually doing in the first year: how they communicate long before words, how attachment forms, and what the science says about crying, sleep, feeding, and play.

By the end of the class, the first year will feel a lot less like guessing.

  • What your baby’s brain is building in the first year, and why responsive care matters more than enrichment.
  • How to read your baby’s signals for ready, hungry, tired, and had enough.
  • How secure attachment forms through everyday moments, and why it doesn’t require perfection.
  • Realistic expectations for crying and sleep, based on the research rather than the pressure.
  • How babies build language before their first words, and how movement and play drive learning.

Four modules for the year when everything is new.

16 short lessons of about ten minutes, easy to watch during a nap. Every lesson has English captions and a full written version under the video.

  1. Module 1

    The Newborn Brain

    5 lessons, about 1 hour

    What newborns can already do, how experience builds the brain, the back-and-forth that shapes it, why babies cry and how to help them calm, and why a baby can be capable and fragile at the same time.

    1. 1Born Ready to Connect
    2. 2A Brain Built by Experience
    3. 3Serve and Return Free to watch
    4. 4States, Crying, and the Borrowed Nervous System
    5. 5Capable and Fragile at Once

  2. Module 2

    Reading Your Baby’s Cues

    4 lessons, about 40 minutes

    The signals that mean ready, hungry, tired, or had enough. Your baby’s temperament, feeding as a two-way conversation, and what sleep really looks like across the first year.

    1. 1The Cue Vocabulary
    2. 2Temperament
    3. 3Feeding as a Conversation
    4. 4Sleep Across the First Year

  3. Module 3

    Attachment in the First Year

    3 lessons, about 30 minutes

    What secure attachment is and isn’t, how babies attach to more than one person (including in childcare), and why separation and stranger wariness show up later in the year.

    1. 1What Secure Attachment Is, and Isn’t
    2. 2Many Hands
    3. 3Separation and Stranger Wariness

  4. Module 4

    The Explorer: Language, Movement, and Play

    4 lessons, about 40 minutes

    Babbling, pointing, and the first words your baby understands. Tummy time, crawling, and how moving changes everything. Play, hiding games, and what the evidence says about screens.

    1. 1Language Before Words
    2. 2On the Move
    3. 3Play and the Hidden Toy
    4. 4Screens, and the Year Ahead

What this class draws on.

Eight of the peer-reviewed studies the lessons are built on, at least one from every module.

  1. Nelson et al. (2007). Cognitive recovery in socially deprived young children. Science, 318, 1937–1940. Module 1, lesson 2
  2. Mesman, van IJzendoorn, & Bakermans-Kranenburg (2009). The many faces of the Still-Face Paradigm: a review and meta-analysis. Developmental Review, 29, 120–162. Module 1, lesson 3
  3. Slagt et al. (2016). Differences in sensitivity to parenting depending on child temperament: a meta-analysis. Psychological Bulletin. doi Module 2, lesson 2
  4. Black & Aboud (2011). Responsive feeding is embedded in a theoretical framework of responsive parenting. Journal of Nutrition. doi Module 2, lesson 3
  5. Madigan et al. (2024). Maternal and paternal sensitivity: key determinants of child attachment security examined through meta-analysis. Psychological Bulletin. doi Module 3, lesson 1
  6. Ahnert, Pinquart, & Lamb (2006). Security of children’s relationships with nonparental care providers: a meta-analysis. Child Development. doi Module 3, lesson 2
  7. Goldstein, King, & West (2003). Social interaction shapes babbling. PNAS. doi Module 4, lesson 1
  8. Strouse et al. (2021). Learning from video: a meta-analysis of the video deficit in children ages 0 to 6 years. Child Development, 92(1), e20–e38. doi Module 4, lesson 4

Who this class is for.

  • You have a baby under 12 months and are drowning in conflicting advice about what they need.
  • You’re not sure how to read your baby’s cues, or whether you’re responding “right.”
  • You want to understand what’s actually happening developmentally, not just what to do this week.
  • Sleep, feeding, and stimulation all feel like high-stakes decisions and you’d like a calmer framework.
  • You want research-backed guidance without the judgment or the scripts.

Who teaches it.

Created and taught by Matthew McArthur, a Child Development Specialist with an M.A. in Developmental Psychology from San Diego State University and a B.A. in Psychology from UCLA. He has coached more than 100 families in Los Angeles, where he also teaches an Infant and Toddler Socialization class. The class draws on peer-reviewed research in brain development, language, and emotion regulation, and it is honest about what that research can and can’t tell us.

Realistic for a normal day

Every recommendation is filtered through actual family life: tired adults, multiple kids, work, daycare pickups, and dinner.

Calm and non-shaming

No scripts to memorize, no suggestion that one slip ruins your child. The tone is the way you’d want a thoughtful friend to talk to you.

A few things parents usually ask first.

How do I start, and how do I get back in?

When you enroll, you create a free account, pay securely with Stripe, and the class opens right away. After that it lives in My classes, which remembers where you left off in every lesson.

How much does the class cost?

The class is $49 one time for lifetime access to all 16 lessons. There is no subscription and nothing renews.

Can I get a refund?

Yes. Within 14 days of buying, and as long as you’ve watched 4 lessons or fewer, you get a full refund: email Matthew with the address you bought with. The free lesson doesn’t count toward the four. A refund ends your access to the class. Want to see the teaching first? Watch “Serve and Return” free, a full lesson from Module 1.

Can I read the lessons instead of watching?

Yes. Every lesson has English captions and a full written version under the video, so you can read it with the sound off.

Do I need special software or equipment?

Nothing special. The class is designed to work on your phone, tablet, or laptop. Self-paced means you watch on your schedule, with no live sessions and no deadlines.

Is this medical or psychological advice?

No. The class is educational. For concerns about your own baby, your pediatrician and your baby’s clinicians are the right people to ask, and the class will help you ask better questions.

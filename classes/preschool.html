When a game gets frustrating

  1. The situation

    Your preschooler can take turns in a game. Then they lose a turn and want to walk away.

  2. What is growing

    Handling frustration takes practice. Even when a child knows the rules, they may need help in the moment.

  3. What to try

    Offer a short, shared pause. “That was frustrating. Want to take a breath with me, then decide what to do?”

Who this is for

Your child is three, four, or five. One minute they are capable and funny, and the next they fall apart over something small. Meanwhile, kindergarten is on the horizon and everyone has an opinion about what they should know by then.

This class shows what the research actually says about this stretch of childhood, in plain language, so you can focus on what matters.

What this class helps with

  1. Big reactions over small things

    Understand what changes in the preschool brain, what self-regulation is, and how adults support it without doing it for the child.

  2. Play, friends, and fights

    See why child-directed play does so much developmental work, and what to do (and not do) when children fight or leave each other out.

  3. Knowing what readiness means

    Learn what kindergarten readiness research actually measures, and why executive function matters far more than academic drills.

How children build their own capacity to calm down

Who teaches it

Created and taught by Matthew McArthur, a Child Development Specialist. At San Diego State University he studied how parent stress, changes at home, screen time, and back-and-forth interaction shape language and thinking from 6 months to 5 years, after working with developmental researchers at UCLA, and he wrote “Why Early Chatter Matters” for Frontiers for Young Minds.

He then spent two years in state-funded early intervention, coaching families with children under 3 who were experiencing delays.

Matthew writes and reviews every lesson. The narration is an AI model of his own voice, reading his script word for word.

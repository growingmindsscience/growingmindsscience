A toddler takes about 2,368 steps an hour. And falls 17 times doing it.

For a century, researchers studied walking by coaxing infants down a straight, flat path. In 2012 Karen Adolph's lab at NYU did something different: they filmed 151 infants aged 12 to 19 months during ordinary free play, then coded every step and every fall from video, frame by frame.

The numbers are enormous. 2,368 steps, 701 metres travelled, and 17 falls - per hour. And infants were only in motion 32.3% of that time. Nearly half of all walking bouts were one to three steps long. This is not practice as anyone would design it.

The part that surprised the researchers: brand-new walkers fell almost twice as often per hour as expert crawlers, yet they were the ones who abandoned crawling. Why trade a stable posture for a precarious one? Because walking took them three times as far. And once you account for how much further walkers travel, the fall gap disappears - roughly 69 steps per fall for walkers, 55 for crawlers, a difference that was not statistically significant. Falling is not the price of walking. It is the price of moving.

Scale those hourly rates across six waking hours and the authors estimate a toddler covers around 14,000 steps, 46 football fields of ground, and 100 falls in a single day. Nobody scheduled it. Nobody drilled it. It came out of play.

If you have ever watched a new walker fall, get up, and stagger off again without a flicker of discouragement, this is what you were watching: a practice regimen so large and so varied that no lesson plan could match it.

Source: Adolph, K. E., Cole, W. G., Komati, M., Garciaguirre, J. S., Badaly, D., Lingeman, J. M., Chan, G. L. Y., & Sotsky, R. B. (2012). How do you learn to walk? Thousands of steps and dozens of falls per day. Psychological Science, 23(11), 1387-1394. Later work by Hoch, O'Grady & Adolph (2019) reports 2,400 to 4,200 steps per hour, confirming the same magnitude.

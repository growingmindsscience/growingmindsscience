# 10 · What to do after you lost it

- **Suggested post date:** Monday, 2026-08-10
- **Type:** Practical activity
- **Related site page:** /tools/repair-after-the-hard-moment
- **Status:** draft, not published

---

You snapped. Your voice went somewhere you did not want it to go, over something that did not warrant it, and now your child is looking at you.

Every parent I have worked with has had this moment. Most of them think it is disqualifying.

The useful frame from the attachment literature is that connection is not a state you maintain without interruption. It ruptures and it gets repaired, constantly, in every relationship. What matters over time is not the absence of rupture. It is whether repair reliably follows.

So repair is the skill, and it is small.

**Wait until you are actually regulated.** Repairing while still activated tends to produce a second rupture. A few minutes is fine. It does not have to be immediate to count.

**Name what happened, simply.** "I yelled. That was too loud." You are describing the event, not narrating your inner life to a three-year-old.

**Own it without loading it onto them.** "I was frustrated, and I got too loud" is repair. "I got too loud because you would not put your shoes on" is a bill.

**Do not require them to make you feel better.** No demand for a hug, no "are we okay?" that they have to answer correctly. Offer, and let them take it or not.

**Then move on.** Extended apology is its own kind of pressure.

The thing being taught here is not that you are perfect. It is that things break and get mended, and that a person who hurt you can come back. That turns out to be a fairly important thing to know.

# 11 · Two kinds of "listening," and only one of them lasts

- **Suggested post date:** Wednesday, 2026-08-12
- **Type:** Research explainer
- **Source:** Kochanska, Aksan & Koenig, *Child Development*, 66(6), 1752-1769 (1995)
- **Status:** draft, not published

---

Grazyna Kochanska, Nazan Aksan and Amy Koenig drew a distinction in 1995 that I think about often. Studying young children with their mothers, they separated two things that look identical from the outside.

**Situational compliance** is a child cooperating without real buy-in. The behavior is genuine. It also depends on the adult staying in the room and staying on it.

**Committed compliance** is a child who has taken the adult's agenda on as their own. They are not tracking whether you are watching, because the rule is not coming from you anymore.

The part worth knowing is what went with the committed kind. Not firmness, and not pressure. What tracked with committed compliance and with early conscience was the quality of the relationship: mothers and children in a warm, mutually responsive groove tended to have children who internalized rules rather than merely submitting to them.

Which reframes the exhausting part of toddlerhood. The daily negotiation is not damage being done to your authority. Under warm and consistent conditions, something is being handed over.

Two honest caveats. This is longitudinal correlational work with families, not a randomized trial in parenting style, so read it as a well-supported pattern and not a lever you pull. And it measured relationship warmth and responsiveness. The further claim that specifically supporting a toddler's autonomy produces the same result is a reasonable reading and it fits the wider developmental picture, but it goes a step past what this study measured, so I am not going to state it as a finding.

Still. On a hard afternoon, "this is how it becomes theirs" is a more accurate story than "I am losing control here."

Kochanska, Aksan & Koenig, *Child Development*, 66(6), 1752-1769 (1995).

# 02 · The most useful part of serve and return is the part with no words in it

- **Suggested post date:** Wednesday, 2026-07-22
- **Type:** Practical activity
- **Related site page:** /articles/serve-and-return
- **Status:** draft, not published

---

"Serve and return" has traveled a long way from the lab, and it usually arrives with the wrong emphasis: say more, narrate everything, fill the room with language.

That is not the finding. The finding is about contingency. The child does something communicative, and the adult responds in a way that connects to what the child was already attending to.

Which means the loop has four parts, and only two of them involve talking.

**1. Notice the serve.** It is often quiet. A look toward the door. A hand on your sleeve. A sound that is not a word yet but is clearly trying to become one.

**2. Return it in the same neighborhood.** They looked at the dog, so the dog is the subject. "You saw the dog. He is running."

**3. Add one thing.** One new word, not a lecture. "Running fast."

**4. Wait.**

Step four is the one that gets dropped, and it is doing more work than it looks like. The wait is what hands the turn back. Without it there is no back-and-forth, only broadcast.

Try counting to five in your head after you respond. It will feel much longer than it is. Some of the time, nothing comes back, and that is fine. Some of the time you will get a sound, a look, a whole attempt at a word that you would have talked over.

This is not a technique to run all day. It is a thing to notice you are already doing, and occasionally do on purpose.

# 08 · Try narrating instead of quizzing for one week

- **Suggested post date:** Wednesday, 2026-08-05
- **Type:** Practical activity
- **Status:** draft, not published

---

Most of us, when we want to help a child's language, reach for questions we already know the answer to.

What color is that? What sound does a cow make? Can you say "banana"? What's this one?

It is well intentioned and it comes from somewhere reasonable. But a test question puts the child in the position of performing, and it narrows the exchange to one right answer. A lot of children respond to it by going quiet, especially the ones already worried about getting it wrong.

The swap is small. Say what is happening instead of asking them to prove they know it.

**Instead of** "what color is the cup?" **try** "you picked the blue cup."
**Instead of** "what's that?" **try** "that's a bulldozer. It's pushing the dirt."
**Instead of** "can you say shoes?" **try** "shoes on. Left one, right one."

You are supplying the same words. You are just not requiring a performance to release them. And you leave room for the child to join in rather than answer.

Two caveats, because I would rather be accurate than tidy. Real questions are great, keep those. And if you enjoy the quiz game and your child does too, that is a fine thing to do together. The point is not to ban a sentence type. It is to notice if questions have become most of what you say, and to have somewhere else to go.

One week is enough to feel the difference in the room.

# 04 · Where the word "scaffolding" came from, and what it actually described

- **Suggested post date:** Monday, 2026-07-27
- **Type:** Research explainer
- **Source:** Wood, Bruner & Ross, *Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry*, 17(2), 89-100 (1976)
- **Status:** draft, not published

---

"Scaffolding" is everywhere in education and parenting content now, usually meaning something vague like "helping."

It started somewhere specific. In 1976, David Wood, Jerome Bruner and Gail Ross watched children between 3 and 5 try to build a pyramid out of interlocking wooden blocks, with a tutor alongside them. The task was chosen to be past what the children could do alone but within reach with help.

What the paper did that mattered was describe what the tutor was actually doing, rather than how much they helped. They named six functions:

**Recruitment.** Getting the child interested in the task at all.
**Reducing degrees of freedom.** Cutting the number of moves the child has to manage, and covering the rest yourself.
**Direction maintenance.** Keeping them pointed at the goal when momentum drops.
**Marking critical features.** Drawing attention to what matters here, and away from what does not.
**Frustration control.** Keeping the difficulty tolerable.
**Demonstration.** Modelling a solution in a form the child can take up, not just doing it for them.

The age pattern in the study is the part I find most useful. The youngest children needed things shown rather than explained, and needed the most help staying in the task at all. The middle group mostly needed prompting and correction. The oldest mostly needed confirmation that they were on track.

So scaffolding is not a fixed amount of help. It is help shaped to what this child cannot yet do, withdrawn as they can, and the skill in it is the withdrawing.

Worth saying plainly: this is a small observational study from 1976, and it named a pattern rather than proving a mechanism. Its value is the vocabulary. Once you can see the six functions, you notice which one you are reaching for when a child is stuck, and whether it is still the one they need.

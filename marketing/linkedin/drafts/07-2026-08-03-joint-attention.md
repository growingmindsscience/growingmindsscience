# 07 · Why following beats leading when a child is learning words

- **Suggested post date:** Monday, 2026-08-03
- **Type:** Research explainer
- **Source:** Tomasello & Farrar, *Child Development*, 57(6), 1454-1463 (1986)
- **Status:** draft, not published

---

There is a small, consistent finding in early language research that I think deserves more attention than it gets.

When an adult labels an object the child is *already looking at*, the word tends to stick. When an adult redirects the child to look at something else and then labels it, it sticks less well.

Michael Tomasello and Michael Jeffrey Farrar reported this pattern in 1986. Before children have much language, they and a caregiver establish a shared focus: both attending to the same thing, and in some sense both aware that they are. Words offered inside that shared focus arrive with the referent already sorted out. The child is not solving "which of the fourteen things in this room is she naming?" That question is already answered.

Redirecting first makes the child do two jobs. Disengage from what interested them, find what interests you, and only then map the word.

This is why the advice "follow the child's lead" is not sentimentality. It is a claim about how much cognitive work is left over for the language part.

What it looks like on a Tuesday: they are staring at the drain. You were going to point out the truck. Do the drain. "Water going down. It's loud."

The truck will still be there.

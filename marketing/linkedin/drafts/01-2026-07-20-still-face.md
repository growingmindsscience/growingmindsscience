# 01 · What happens when a parent's face goes blank for two minutes

- **Suggested post date:** Monday, 2026-07-20
- **Type:** Research explainer
- **Sources:** Tronick, Als, Adamson, Wise & Brazelton, *Journal of the American Academy of Child Psychiatry*, 17(1), 1-13 (1978); Mesman, van IJzendoorn & Bakermans-Kranenburg, *Developmental Review*, 29(2), 120-162 (2009)
- **Status:** draft, not published

---

In 1978, Edward Tronick and colleagues published a procedure that has been run thousands of times since, because it shows something about babies that is hard to see any other way.

A parent and an infant play face to face, normally. Then the parent is asked to stop. Keep looking at the baby, but hold a still, neutral expression. No smiling, no talking, no response. Two minutes. Then normal interaction resumes.

Babies do not sit quietly through it. They try to restart the exchange. They smile, they point, they call out, they reach. When that does not work, most of them look away, and their distress goes up. Then, when the parent comes back online, they recover.

The finding is that infants are not just receiving interaction. They have expectations about it, and they act to repair it when it breaks.

This one has held up, which is why I am comfortable posting it. Judi Mesman and colleagues reviewed and meta-analysed more than 80 still-face studies in 2009 and found the core effect consistently: less positive affect and less gaze during the still-face period, more negative affect, with some of it carrying over into the reunion.

What I would ask you not to take from it: that a distracted moment harms your baby. The procedure is deliberately extreme and deliberately sustained, which is exactly why it produces a visible effect. Real life has phone calls, other children, and bad days in it, and it also has the reunion, which is the part of the paradigm babies reliably recover from.

The useful version is smaller. Your baby is already treating you as someone who responds. That expectation is the foundation the rest of it gets built on, and it was there before either of you did anything on purpose.

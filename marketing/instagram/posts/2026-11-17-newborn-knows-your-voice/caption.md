# Caption

Your newborn already knows your voice. Under three days old, and already working to hear you. The study, plainly.

In 1980, DeCasper and Fifer tested ten babies under three days old. Each sucked on a pacifier wired so that the rhythm of its sucking switched on either the mother's voice or a stranger's, both reading the same Dr. Seuss story.

The babies worked it out. Eight of the ten changed the timing of their sucking to hear their own mother, and kept her voice playing, with at most twelve hours of contact since birth.

The honest catch: ten babies is a small study with one method. What makes it hold up is what came after. Newborns prefer a story their mother read aloud before birth over one she did not, and fetuses respond to her voice with measurable heart-rate changes. The ear starts learning in the last trimester.

So by birth your voice is already the most familiar sound in the world. Ordinary talking, reading aloud and singing are not an introduction. They are a continuation.

# Caption

Do baby word videos build vocabulary? Babies 12 to 18 months watched one for 4 weeks and learned no more words than babies who never saw it.

Maybe it was a gift and it plays while you cook. Researchers tested whether videos like that teach words.

In a randomized study (DeLoache et al., 2010), babies who watched a popular word DVD for 4 weeks learned no more words than babies who never saw it. The most learning came from parents teaching the words during everyday routines.

If someone in your family is sure the video is working, they are in good company. In the study, the parents who liked the DVD thought so too.

Robb et al. (2009) found the same in babies 12 to 15 months: 6 weeks of a word DVD, no added vocabulary growth. Across 122 results from 59 reports on children up to age 6, Strouse et al. (2021) found children learned clearly less from video than from a live person (about half a standard deviation, in the researchers' terms), and the gap shrank as they got older.

The honest catch: the two baby trials tested word DVDs and word learning only, not every kind of screen use, and the review's authors note that some studies may overstate the gap. This is not about guilt over a show while you cook.

Things to try tonight: in the bath, hold up the cup, say "cup", and wait for your baby's turn. Do the same with the spoon at dinner and the sock while you dress them.

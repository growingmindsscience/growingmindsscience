# Caption

Toddler tantrum on the checkout floor? It has two parts, anger first and sadness after, and the second part reaches for you.

In Potegal and Davidson (2003), the parents of 335 children aged 18 months to 5 years wrote down each tantrum soon after it ended. The most common length was half a minute to a minute, and 75% were over within 5 minutes.

Then the researchers looked at what children did in each half minute and found two things running at once. Anger (screaming, kicking, throwing) peaked at the start and fell away. Distress (whining, crying, reaching for comfort) built slowly underneath it and outlasted it.

The honest catch: the times come from parents' own accounts, not a stopwatch.

Something to try: in the anger, when almost nothing reaches your child, stay close and keep it quiet. When the crying turns into reaching for you, that is the sadness, and your moment to comfort them.

If your child's tantrums come out of nowhere, go on and on, break things, or come many times a day for weeks, that is a different pattern, and worth asking your pediatrician about.

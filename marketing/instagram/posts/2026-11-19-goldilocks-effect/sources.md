# Sources

Verified from the open-access primary source for the 2026-08-31 carousel (built and verified, never posted); rebuilt here with the same claims.

- Kidd C, Piantadosi ST, Aslin RN. The Goldilocks effect: human infants allocate attention to visual sequences that are neither too simple nor too complex. *PLOS ONE*. 2012;7(5):e36399. Two experiments, n = 72 in total, 7- and 8-month-olds; look-away criterion one second; ideal-learner model; U-shaped attention with the sweet spot near 1.25 bits.
- Kidd C, Piantadosi ST, Aslin RN. The Goldilocks effect in infant auditory attention. *Child Development*. 2014;85(5):1795-1804.
- Piantadosi ST, Kidd C, Aslin R. Rich analysis and rational models: inferring individual behavior from infant looking data. *Developmental Science*. 2014;17(3):321-337. The within-infant confirmation.
- Poli F, Serino G, Mars RB, Hunnius S. Infants tailor their attention to maximize learning. *Science Advances*. 2020;6(39):eabb5053.

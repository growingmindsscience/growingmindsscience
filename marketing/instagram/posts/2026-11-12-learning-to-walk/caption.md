# Caption

A toddler takes about 2,368 steps an hour. And falls 17 times doing it. Here is the study, plainly.

For a century, researchers studied walking by coaxing infants down a straight, flat path. In 2012 Karen Adolph's lab at NYU did something different: they filmed 151 infants aged 12 to 19 months during ordinary free play, then coded every step and every fall from video, frame by frame.

The numbers are enormous. 2,368 steps, 701 meters traveled and 17 falls, per hour, and the infants were only in motion about a third of that time. Nearly half of all walking bouts were one to three steps long. This is not practice as anyone would design it.

The part that surprised the researchers: brand-new walkers fell almost twice as often per hour as expert crawlers, yet they were the ones who abandoned crawling. Why trade a stable posture for a precarious one? Because walking took them three times as far, and once you account for distance, the fall gap disappears. Falling is not the price of walking. It is the price of moving.

The honest catch: one lab, one culture, one age window. Later work from the same group puts the hourly step count even higher, so the magnitude holds.

Scale it across six waking hours and a toddler covers around 14,000 steps and 100 falls in a day. Nobody scheduled it. It came out of play.

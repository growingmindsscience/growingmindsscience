# Caption

18 months and not saying much yet? Here is the range, and the few things actually worth a conversation.

First words often arrive around the first birthday, with wide normal variation. By 18 months most children try to say three or more words beyond "mama" and "dada". Understanding runs well ahead of speech the whole time, so a child who says little but follows "bring me the cup" is usually a child on a wide, ordinary path.

At this age the signs of communication are mostly not words. Pointing to show you something. Looking where you look. Taking turns with sounds. Following a simple instruction without a gesture. Those matter more than the count.

Worth raising with your pediatrician: not pointing or gesturing to share, not responding to their name most of the time, very little eye contact or back-and-forth, words that have disappeared, or a consistent lag across several areas rather than one. None of that is a diagnosis. All of it is a good reason to ask, and asking early is never overreacting. When there is a genuine delay, support that starts earlier tends to work better.

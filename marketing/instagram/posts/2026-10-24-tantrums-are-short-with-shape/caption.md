# Caption

Toddler tantrum on the checkout floor? It feels endless, but the most common tantrum lasts 30 seconds to a minute.

In Potegal and Davidson (2003), parents of 335 children aged 18 months to 5 years described tantrums soon after they happened. The most common length was half a minute to a minute, and 75% were over within 5 minutes. Anger peaked early and faded, while distress rose slowly underneath it.

Among nearly 1,500 preschoolers in Wakschlag and colleagues (2012), 83.7% had tantrums sometimes and 8.6% had them daily, fewer than 1 in 10. The behaviors that concerned the researchers were unpredictable, prolonged or destructive. If your child's tantrums come out of nowhere, go on and on, break things, or come many times a day for weeks, that is a different pattern, and worth asking your pediatrician about.

The honest catch: the durations come from parents' own descriptions, not timed recordings.

Something to try: stay close and keep it quiet through the first minute, when almost nothing reaches your child. When the crying turns into reaching for you, that is your moment to comfort them.

One detail not to over-read: tantrums with an early drop to the floor were shorter. That is a correlation, so do not try to steer it.

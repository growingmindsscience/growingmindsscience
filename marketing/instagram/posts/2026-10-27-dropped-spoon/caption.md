# Caption

She dropped the spoon again. Ninth time. Here is what may be growing while you pick it up.

At about 8 to 14 months, letting go on purpose is a new skill, and a thing that vanishes over the edge and lands with a sound is worth repeating. But watch where her eyes go the moment it lands. Usually to your face. The drop is half the game. What you do next is the other half.

Any bid for your attention is a serve: a look, a sound, a point, a spoon on the floor. A word back ("spoon, down it goes") and a pause for her to answer is the return. That ordinary exchange, repeated across a day, is one of the best-described ways attention and language get built. It does not need to be cheerful every time. It needs to happen more often than not.

Three rounds is plenty. Then the spoon stays on the tray and you say "done".

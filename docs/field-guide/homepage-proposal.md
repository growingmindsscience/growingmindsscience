# Homepage proposal

The page runs header, nine sections, footer. Copy comes from `copy-deck.md`; geometry from the printed annual direction.

## Header

Claim: the whole site is one click away without a decision tree. Proof: five links (Explore, Classes, Tools, Articles, About), My classes, and one action, "Enroll, from $49". Composition: a paper bar; the Tools menu opens with a 6px forest top edge and lists every tool in order. Links: every product, so nothing existing is orphaned.

## 1. Hero

Claim: behavior has a developmental process beneath it. Proof: the masthead plate, a baby dropping a spoon, with four annotations (an experiment, a serve, where did it go, the return). Composition: a full-width plate band, then the title on the 42% axis with the standfirst and actions beside the four notes, which sit in the margin zone to the left. Interaction: each note is a button that reveals one sentence and lights the matching callout on the plate; the plate opens as a shutter on load. Links: Explore a moment (to the Lens), Explore by age, and a quiet line to /tools/right-now.

## 2. Lens preview

Claim: you can learn a way of looking, not a script. Proof: the Developmental Lens working on leaving the playground, headed with the question parents ask ("Why is leaving the playground so hard?"). Composition: a heading with a short introduction beside it, then the visit as four frames running wide: absorbed in play, "time to go", the run back, on the floor. Each frame pairs its photograph with the scene, what may be developing beneath it, and what helps at that moment. Interaction: a sideways carousel with a four-stop rail and arrows that follow the scroll; no tabs. The four-step Observe, Understand, Respond, Reflect structure lives on the full Lens page. Links: the full Lens page at /lens/leaving-the-playground, and one Read, Try and Learn line under the carousel.

## 3. Explore by age

Claim: the first five years are five different jobs. Proof: five stages, each with what is growing, what helps, and one article, tool and class. Composition: a photograph in the margin zone, the two-line heading on the axis, the stage agenda running wide. Interaction: an age cursor (birth to 5 years) selects the stage tab; the tabs also work directly. Without JavaScript every stage is shown, stacked. Links: Browse the classes, plus per-stage articles, tools and classes.

## 4. Three things

Claim: understanding has three parts: your child, yourself, your influence. Proof: one sentence each, the 30% synchrony figure from the infant class, and a four-line list of outcomes. Composition: a mosaic of unlike tiles in paper, sage and ink with one photograph tile, butted on 8px gaps. Interaction: none beyond the scroll drift on the photograph. Links: none; it frames the sections below.

## 5. Moments that cannot wait

Claim: a parent in distress needs a short path, not the whole guide. Proof: a two-minute tool with five named situations. Composition: a whole page of sage carrying one sentence and one action, the situations as plain links. Links: /tools/right-now.

## 6. Classes

Claim: the science is available in depth, once, at a stated price. Proof: a four-column table (three $49 classes and the family class in development) giving lessons, hours, what you can explain afterwards, and a free lesson. Composition: a large table with tone-banded rows; on narrow screens each row becomes a block. Links: the Enroll buttons, the free lesson, the waitlist, the refund terms.

## 7. Tools

Claim: free tools are organized by what you need. Proof: ten rows, from "Hard right now" to "A five-minute break". Composition: a two-line statement, then a photograph at left and the table pushed right. Links: every tool, including the Number Path and the Claims library.

## 8. Trust

Claim: the author has done this work. Proof: a biography naming San Diego State University, UCLA, Frontiers for Young Minds and early intervention, with three facts: San Diego, sources cited on every page, educational and not clinical. Composition: the portrait as a drop capital in a large paragraph, the facts in the margin zone. Links: none in the section; About is in the header and footer.

## 9. Final invitation

Claim: the idea, again, inverted. Proof: the headline "Understand the mind behind the moment" and one sentence. Composition: a full ink field at least 90svh tall, headline at 9.4vw, sentence and button on one baseline. Links: Explore a moment.

## Footer

Four link columns (Classes, Tools, About, Elsewhere) above a full-width wordmark on the same ink field, with the educational-only legal line.

## Visitor journeys

**Curious.** Reads the title, taps "A serve" on the plate and reads one sentence. Chooses Explore a moment, steps through the Lens to Understand and selects a callout, then follows a Read link. Later reaches the explorer, sets the cursor to their child's age, and meets the three things.

**Overwhelmed.** Arrives mid-moment. The quiet line under the hero actions, "Something hard happening right now? Start here", goes to /tools/right-now. If they scroll instead, section 5 offers the same path on a plain sage page with five situations named.

**Committed.** Uses the explorer to find the stage and follows its class link, or scrolls to the classes table to compare lessons, hours and outcomes. The trust section is read before the price decision; the free lesson and the 14-day refund line sit at the point of choice. The close repeats the way back into the Lens.

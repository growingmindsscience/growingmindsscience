Milestone Navigator

Worried about something specific: talking, walking, hearing, play? Pick the worry, answer a few plain questions, and get a clear, research-grounded next step, whether that’s genuine reassurance or exactly what to say when you call.

Tools · Free · Birth to 5 · A few minutes

Start with your worry How it works

Honest about its job

What this is, and isn’t

  • It turns a vague worry into specific observations, and observations into a next step.
  • Reassurance here is real: nothing you reported crosses any of the research-backed lines this tool checks, and a clear result lists the questions it rests on.
  • It is not a screening or diagnostic instrument, and it never labels your child.
  • Everything stays on this device. Your answers are never sent to us.

This tool offers educational information, not medical advice, diagnosis, or screening. It cannot see your child; it can only organize what you report. Decisions about your child’s health belong with you and your child’s clinician. If your child is in immediate danger or distress, call your local emergency number.

How this works

Every path through this tool is checked against a set of red lines drawn from published guidance, like not walking by 18 months (AAP motor-delay guidance) or caregiver concern about hearing at any age (Joint Committee on Infant Hearing). An automated check runs on every update to this site and enumerates every possible path: if any answer pattern that crosses one of these lines would land on a softer result than that line allows, the check fails and flags the change. The tool can be more careful than these lines; it is never allowed to be less.

Loss of skills a child once had is treated as the most important observation at any age, in every category. If a change came on suddenly, over hours or a day or two, a Talk soon result says so plainly: weakness on one side, a drooping face, trouble speaking or walking, or a seizure means calling your local emergency number now, and any other sudden change means calling your pediatrician today. And hearing is deliberately different from everything else here: your concern alone, or responses to sound that are missing or patchy, is enough for a hearing test, with no watch-and-wait step in between.

Some areas have few questions at the youngest and oldest ages. When that is the case, a clear result says so, instead of reading as a full check.

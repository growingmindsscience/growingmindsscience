Before your child’s checkup

Write down what you’ve noticed and what you want to ask, then print one page to bring to the visit. Checkups are short, and it’s easy to forget the question you meant to ask.

Tools · Free · Birth to 5 · About 5 minutes

What this is, and isn’t

Your notes, organized

  • It turns what you’ve noticed and what you want to ask into one page for the visit.
  • It never scores, rates, or flags anything about your child.
  • It is not a screening or diagnosis.
  • Everything stays on this device. Nothing you write is sent to us.

If you’re worried, you don’t have to wait for the visit.

You can call your pediatrician now. If your child has stopped doing something they used to do, like saying words they had, babbling, waving, or walking, call and ask to be seen soon rather than saving it for the checkup. For a child under 3, you can also contact your state’s early intervention program yourself; from age 3, your local public school district. No referral is needed. What to do if you’re concerned (CDC).

This page offers educational information, not medical advice, screening, or diagnosis. It organizes what you write; it cannot see your child.

How this works

Well visits are when your child’s doctor checks on development: how your child is moving, talking, playing, and getting along. The Bright Futures schedule from the American Academy of Pediatrics sets checkups after the newborn exam in the hospital at 3 to 5 days, 1, 2, 4, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 24, and 30 months, and then once a year. The Academy recommends that doctors ask about development at every well visit, use a developmental screening questionnaire at 9, 18, and 30 months, and use an autism-specific one at 18 and 24 months. It also recommends asking new mothers about postpartum depression at the 1, 2, 4, and 6 month visits. Your notes help that conversation start from what you see every day.

If low mood, worry, or irritability lasts more than two weeks, during pregnancy or in the year after a birth, tell your doctor or your baby’s pediatrician. In the US, the National Maternal Mental Health Hotline is free and confidential, any time of day.

If your child was born more than 3 weeks early (from 4 to 18 weeks), the page shows corrected age (counted from the due date) under 24 months, the way your pediatrician does and the way our Communication Snapshot does. Your draft is kept in this browser until you clear it, so you can add to it over a few days.

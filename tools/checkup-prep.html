Before your child’s checkup

Write down what you’ve noticed and what you want to ask, then print one page to bring to the visit. Checkups are short, and it’s easy to forget the question you meant to ask.

Tools · Free · Birth to 5 · About 5 minutes

Start your page How it works

What this is, and isn’t

Your notes, organized

  • It turns what you’ve noticed and what you want to ask into one page for the visit.
  • It never scores, rates, or flags anything about your child.
  • It is not a screening or diagnosis.
  • Everything stays on this device. Nothing you write is sent to us.

If you’re worried, you don’t have to wait for the visit.

You can call your pediatrician now. For a child under 3, you can also contact your state’s early intervention program yourself; from age 3, your local public school district. No referral is needed. What to do if you’re concerned (CDC).

This page offers educational information, not medical advice, screening, or diagnosis. It organizes what you write; it cannot see your child.

How this works

Well visits are when your child’s doctor checks on development: how your child is moving, talking, playing, and getting along. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that doctors ask about development at every well visit, use a developmental screening questionnaire at 9, 18, and 30 months, and use an autism-specific one at 18 and 24 months. Your notes help that conversation start from what you see every day.

If your child was born more than 3 weeks early, the page shows corrected age (counted from the due date) under 24 months, the way your pediatrician does. Your draft is kept in this browser until you clear it, so you can add to it over a few days.

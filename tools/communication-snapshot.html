Communication Snapshot

A few careful questions about words, gestures, and understanding. You get a calm, research-grounded read on where your child's communication sits right now, and printable notes if a pediatrician conversation is worth having.

Start the snapshot How it works
What this is, and isn't

Honest about its job

  • It organizes what you already observe into the shape a pediatrician conversation actually uses.
  • Every line it draws is tied to published research, and it never softens one.
  • It is not a screening or diagnostic instrument, and it never labels your child.
  • Everything stays on this device. Your answers are never sent to us.

This tool offers educational information, not medical advice, diagnosis, or screening. It cannot see your child; it can only organize what you report. Decisions about your child's health belong with you and your child's clinician.

How this works

Behind this tool sits a set of published red lines, things like no single words by 16 months (Filipek et al., 1999) and the fewer than 50 words or no combinations at 24 months late-talker marker (Rescorla, 1989). Each line is stored with its source, and an automated check runs on every update to this site: if a change would ever classify a red-line observation more softly than the research supports, the update fails and cannot ship. The tool can be more careful than the literature; it is never allowed to be less.

Two details we handle the way clinicians do: if your child was born more than 3 weeks early, we use corrected age (counted from the due date) under 24 months. And words count across every language your family speaks; being bilingual never delays any line here.

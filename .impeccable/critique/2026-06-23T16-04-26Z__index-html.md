## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated? No.** This is clearly hand-crafted with a real POV: a named "Tidepool" theme, custom SVG orbital-ring + botanical-sprig decor, a signature typographic move (coral serif-italic `.hl` inside grotesque headlines), a bespoke interactive "Growth Arc" tablist for the five developmental stages, an embedded chat demo, and an arch/doorway radius motif for hero imagery. Passes the brand slop test and the second-order category-reflex check (a parenting site that is teal+coral "Tidepool" with Bricolage Grotesque — neither pastel mommy-blog nor clinical).

**Deterministic scan** (`detect.mjs`, exit 2, 3 findings):
- `design-system-font` (warning, line 25): Bricolage Grotesque not in DESIGN.md — true, but an artifact of DESIGN.md being stale (documents styles.css). Fix by re-documenting Tidepool, not by changing the page.
- `single-font` (warning): **false positive** — the page genuinely pairs Bricolage Grotesque (display) + Source Serif 4 (body) via CSS vars in home.css; the scanner only saw the HTML.
- `numbered-section-markers` (advisory): `01–05` modules (a legitimate ordered sequence — the skill's own carve-out) plus the "Four ways" `1–4` (a non-sequence; weaker justification).

**Visual evidence** (1280×900 full-page screenshot): below the hero, the page rendered **blank** — Trust band text, Growth Arc, flagship class, AI demo, and Four-ways sections were all invisible; only hero and footer painted. Cause confirmed in source: `home.css:957` sets `html.anim [data-animate]{opacity:0}` and only `.is-visible` (added by IntersectionObserver, `home.js:121`) reveals it. No-JS and reduced-motion paths stay visible (good), but JS-on + no-scroll renderers never trigger the observer.

## Overall Impression

A genuinely well-crafted, distinctive landing page with a clear voice and strong accessibility scaffolding (skip link, single h1, ARIA tablist, labeled fields, aria-live regions). The biggest opportunity is robustness, not aesthetics: content visibility is gated behind a scroll-reveal, so a whole class of non-scrolling renderers (social/OG preview, screenshots, print/PDF, reader mode) see blank sections. Secondary opportunity: dial back two formulaic mannerisms (per-section eyebrows, highlight-italic on every heading) so the genuine craft reads as intentional rather than templated.

## What's Working

1. **A real, named visual identity.** "Tidepool" is committed and coherent — teal-ink + coral on aqua-mist, with a signature coral serif-italic highlight. This is voice, not decoration.
2. **Accessibility is taken seriously.** Skip link, semantic landmarks, single h1, a correctly-wired `role="tablist"` Growth Arc with `aria-selected`/`tabindex` management, labeled form fields, `aria-live` status regions, descriptive alt text, and a real reduced-motion fallback.
3. **Trust is earned, not asserted.** Founder photo + credentials in the hero, a research-grounded quote band ("50+ years of peer-reviewed research"), transparent pricing ($49, lifetime), and a "never medical advice" line. Matches the brand's calm-credibility goal.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Content ships blank in non-scrolling renderers
- **Why it matters**: `html.anim [data-animate]{opacity:0}` + IntersectionObserver reveal means social/OG preview bots, full-page screenshot tools, print-to-PDF, and reader modes that render with JS but don't scroll get blank below-fold sections — including the entire flagship-class pitch and the waitlist. Hurts shareability and some crawler snapshots. Real scrolling users are unaffected.
- **Fix**: Keep the reveal as enhancement, but add a failsafe so content can never stay hidden: a `setTimeout` (~1.2–1.5s) in `home.js` that adds `.is-visible` to any remaining `[data-animate]`, and/or reveal on `window.load` when nothing has intersected. Optionally widen the IntersectionObserver `rootMargin` so near-fold elements reveal earlier. The default rest state should be visible; animation should layer on top.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable animate` (rework the reveal to enhance an already-visible default) or `/impeccable harden`.

### [P2] Eyebrow kicker on every section
- **Why it matters**: Six sections each open with a small tracked `.eyebrow` ("Developmental science · Ages 0–5", "The first five years", "The flagship class", "Growing Minds AI · Free…", "What's here", "Now enrolling"). A uniform per-section eyebrow is the AI-grammar cadence the skill flags — even when individually crafted, the reflex shows in the repetition.
- **Fix**: Keep eyebrows only where they carry real information (e.g. "Free · Answers show their sources", pricing/age cues) and drop or vary the rest, so the cadence isn't identical down the page. Let some sections open straight on the headline.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable typeset` or `/impeccable quieter`.

### [P2] Two "Enroll now" links, two destinations
- **Why it matters**: The nav "Enroll now" and hero "See the toddler class" go to `/classes/toddlerhood.html` (internal detail page), while the class-rail "Enroll now" and signup "Enroll in the toddler class" jump to an external Thinkific URL. Same/again-similar label, different behavior — a consistency violation that can disorient a buyer mid-decision.
- **Fix**: Make the label predict the destination. Reserve "Enroll now" for the action that actually starts checkout (Thinkific); use "See the class" / "Class details" for the internal page. Or route all enroll CTAs through one consistent path.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`.

### [P2] Muted-text contrast needs verification on tinted/dark surfaces
- **Why it matters**: `home.css` annotates `--ink-muted #5C7472` as AA "for ≥14px" only, and `--on-dark-muted #8BA59F` sits on the pine bands. Anything below 14px using these (meta, captions, footer fine print, the chat "online · free" label) risks failing 4.5:1, which undercuts the stated WCAG AA bar.
- **Fix**: Audit every use of `--ink-muted` / `--on-dark-muted` / `--on-dark-soft` at its real rendered size against its actual background; bump toward ink/on-dark for any small text below 4.5:1.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable audit`.

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer)**: Mostly served well — plain language, labeled nav, obvious first action. Red flag: clicking "Enroll now" in the nav lands on a detail page, not checkout, while the same words elsewhere jump to Thinkific; a first-timer reads that as a dead end or a bug.

**Riley (Stress Tester)**: Shares the page link in Slack/iMessage → the OG render and any link-preview screenshot show blank below-fold sections (same P1 cause). Saving to PDF or using reader mode reproduces it. The happy path (scroll in a real browser) is solid.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile)**: Seven stacked sections make a long one-handed scroll; primary "Enroll"/CTA is repeated, which helps. Decor SVGs have mobile size/opacity media queries, so they shouldn't crowd content — worth a real-device check on the Growth Arc tablist (horizontal tab row in a narrow viewport).

**Tired/Skeptical Parent (project persona, from PRODUCT.md)**: Well matched — research grounding is front-loaded (trust band, sources-shown AI, "never medical advice"), tone is calm not urgent. Risk: density. An exhausted skimmer meets a lot of copy; the clear headings + `.hl` emphasis carry them, but tightening section count or copy would lower cognitive load.

## Minor Observations

- "Four ways" numbered `1–4` is decorative numbering on a set, not a sequence; the modules `01–05` numbering is justified (ordered curriculum). Consider dropping numerals on "Four ways".
- The `.hl` coral serif-italic appears on nearly every heading ("Here's why", "five seasons", "explained", "curated science library", "live."). It's a real signature move, but at this frequency it edges toward a tic — reserve it for the headlines that most deserve the emphasis.
- Hero credibility paragraph is small muted text over a busy decor area — confirm it clears contrast at its rendered size.

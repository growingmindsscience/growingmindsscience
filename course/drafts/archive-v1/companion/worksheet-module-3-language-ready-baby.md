# Worksheet · Module 3 · The Language-Ready Baby

**What this is for:** a place to record what happens when you try the five "Try
this week" activities from Module 3, all of which are about the shape of your
interaction rather than the amount of it.

Nothing on this page asks you to talk more. The evidence in Module 3 points at
contingency and direction: speech aimed at your baby, about what your baby is
already attending to, arriving in response to something they did. There is no
quota here, and there is nothing to count.

---

## 1. The three-second pause

**What to do.** Several times a day, when your baby is calm, alert, and looking
at you, say one short thing to them. Then stop, keep looking at them, keep your
face open, and silently count to three. Then respond to whatever they did, as
though they had said something, and pause again.

**Write it down.**

What you said: ___________________________________________________

What came back (coo, syllable, kick, eyebrow, mouth opening, nothing):

__________________________________________________________________

How many turns you got: [ ] 0  [ ] 1  [ ] 2  [ ] 3 or more

How long three seconds felt: [ ] fine  [ ] long  [ ] unbearable

**What you are looking for:** anything that fills the slot. A coo, a syllable, a
mouth opening, a kick, a sharp intake of breath, an eyebrow.

**What it means if nothing happens:** usually that they are not in the right
state. Vocal exchanges happen in the calm-alert window, which is narrow in young
babies and can be minutes long in a whole day. Try again after a nap or in the
middle of a diaper change, which is a reliably face-to-face moment. If nothing
comes back for weeks and there is also no reaction to sound, see the clinician
note at the end of Module 3.

---

## 2. Answer the babble with words, not just noise

**What to do.** When your baby produces a syllable, respond promptly with real
speech that picks up what they did. If they say "ba," you might say "ba! Yeah,
the ball," rather than only smiling or only echoing "ba ba ba" back.

**Write it down.**

Sounds they are making right now: ________________________________

__________________________________________________________________

What you said back, once: ________________________________________

Over several days, did the sounds start including more consonants?

[ ] yes  [ ] no  [ ] too early to tell

**What you are looking for:** over days, not minutes, whether they vocalize more
when you do this, and whether the sounds start including more consonants.
Goldstein and Schwade found infants restructuring toward what they heard in the
response.

**What it means if nothing happens:** at three months, nothing should happen yet,
because consonant-vowel babbling has not arrived. Under six months this activity
is mostly for the turn-taking, not the sounds. It is not a test.

---

## 3. Aim your talking, once a day, on purpose

**What to do.** Pick one routine you already do, such as a bath, a change, or
unpacking groceries, and talk through it to your baby's face rather than over
their head. Name the thing they are actually looking at, when they are looking at
it.

**Write it down.**

Routine chosen: __________________________________________________

What they were looking at: _______________________________________

What you named: __________________________________________________

Did their gaze come to your face and go back to the object?

[ ] yes  [ ] no  [ ] not sure

**What you are looking for:** their gaze coming to your face and going back to
the object, and eventually their eyes going to the thing you named.

**What it means if nothing happens:** following someone else's attention develops
across the second half of the first year and is not expected early. In young
babies, look for face-watching instead. Persistent lack of eye contact with
familiar people is worth raising.

---

## 4. Turn the background off for twenty minutes

**What to do.** Once a day, kill the TV, radio, or podcast during a period when
you are with your baby. You are not doing this for silence. You are doing it so
that the speech that exists is speech aimed at them.

**Write it down.**

What you turned off: _____________________________________________

Did you talk to your baby more? [ ] yes  [ ] no  [ ] about the same

How the room felt: _______________________________________________

Days I skipped this, and that was fine: __________________________

**What you are looking for:** honestly, mostly a change in you. Most people talk
to the baby more when nothing else is talking.

**What it means if nothing happens:** nothing is wrong. This is an adjustment to
your own environment, not a test of your baby. Skip it on the days when the
background noise is what is keeping you upright.

---

## 5. Use your own language, out loud

**What to do.** If you speak a language other than the community language, use it
with your baby, including songs and phrases you learned as a child.

**Write it down.**

Language or languages I actually speak: __________________________

A song or phrase from my own childhood I used this week: _________

__________________________________________________________________

Which language I am most fluent and comfortable in: ______________

**What you are looking for:** your own fluency and comfort, which is the thing
that actually varies the input quality.

**What it means if nothing happens:** there is nothing to look for in the short
term. This is included because parents are routinely and wrongly advised to drop
a heritage language to protect the community language. There is no good evidence
supporting that advice.

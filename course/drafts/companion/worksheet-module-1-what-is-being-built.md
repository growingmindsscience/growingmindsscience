# Worksheet · Module 1 · What Is Actually Being Built

**What this is for:** a place to write down what you notice while you do the five
"Try this week" activities from Module 1, so the ideas in the module become
something you have observed rather than something you have read.

Module 1 is the module that takes something off your plate. Your baby's brain is
built for intake, it runs on ordinary human input, and the thing that carries the
most weight is contingency: your response depending on what your baby just did.
None of the activities below are tests, none of them have to happen on a
schedule, and skipping one costs nothing.

---

## 1. The eight inch face

**What to do.** Once a day, when your baby is calm and alert, hold them at
roughly the distance from your chest to your face, around eight to twelve inches,
and just look at them. Talk if you like. Do not perform.

**Write it down.**

Day and rough time: ______________________________________________

What their eyes did: _____________________________________________

What their body did (movement, breathing): _______________________

__________________________________________________________________

Anything else you noticed: _______________________________________

__________________________________________________________________

**What you are looking for:** sustained gaze, quieting of movement, changes in
expression, possibly a widening of the eyes or a slowing of breathing. In older
babies, smiles and vocalizations.

**What it means if nothing happens:** very likely a state issue rather than a
skill issue. Newborns are only in a quiet alert state for short stretches, and a
baby who is drowsy, hungry, or overstimulated will disengage. Try a different
point in the day. Persistent lack of any visual response over weeks, or eyes that
never seem to fix on anything, is worth mentioning to your pediatrician, which is
a screening question, not an alarm.

---

## 2. Count one rally

**What to do.** Once this week, when your baby makes any sound or gesture,
respond, then wait. Count how many back and forth turns you get before it ends.
That is all. You are not trying to extend it.

**Write it down.**

Number of turns: [ ] 1  [ ] 2  [ ] 3  [ ] 4 or more

What started it (sound, gesture, expression): ____________________

How long you waited before filling the silence: __________________

What ended it: ___________________________________________________

**What you are looking for:** any turn taking at all. Two exchanges is a real
rally. Newborns may manage only one. Babies around three to four months often
manage several.

**What it means if nothing happens:** turn taking emerges gradually and is
heavily state dependent. Also check your own pacing: adults routinely respond
faster than an infant can process, which ends the rally by accident. Try leaving
a genuinely uncomfortable pause, several seconds, before you fill the silence.

---

## 3. Follow, do not lead

**What to do.** For five minutes, notice what your baby is already looking at,
and talk about that thing. Do not introduce a toy. Do not redirect. Whatever has
their attention is the topic.

**Write it down.**

What had their attention: ________________________________________

What you said about it: __________________________________________

__________________________________________________________________

Did they look back at you? [ ] yes  [ ] no  [ ] not sure

How hard it was to resist redirecting: [ ] easy  [ ] hard  [ ] very hard

**What you are looking for:** longer attention to the object than usual, glances
back at you, or increased vocalizing. You may notice they hold the gaze longer
when you narrate it.

**What it means if nothing happens:** nothing is wrong. Young infants often have
no sustained external focus at all, and this exercise gets much easier around
four to six months. The point of the exercise is partly to retrain you, and that
part works regardless of the response.

---

## 4. Notice the input already there

**What to do.** Pick one routine you do every day, a feed, a nappy change, a
bath, and pay attention to how much serve and return it already contains without
any effort from you.

**Write it down.**

Routine chosen: __________________________________________________

Eye contact happened: [ ] yes  [ ] no

I narrated without meaning to: [ ] yes  [ ] no

I responded to a sound or movement they made: [ ] yes  [ ] no

One exchange from this routine, written out: _____________________

__________________________________________________________________

**What you are looking for:** eye contact, your automatic narration, your
responses to their sounds and movements, the timing of it.

**What it means if nothing happens:** if a routine feels flat or purely
functional, that is normal and it is not a failure. Some feeds are just feeds.
Some days you are running on nothing. The routines are not a performance and they
do not all need to be rich.

---

## 5. Put one thing down

**What to do.** Identify one developmental worry you are currently carrying,
write it in a sentence, and ask two questions of it. Is this about the absence of
something basic, or about the absence of something optimal? And is there an
actual clinical question inside it?

**Write it down.**

The worry, in one sentence: ______________________________________

__________________________________________________________________

Is it about something basic being absent, or something optimal being absent?

[ ] basic  [ ] optimal

Is there a clinical question inside it? [ ] yes  [ ] no

If yes, the question I will ask my pediatrician: _________________

__________________________________________________________________

**What you are looking for:** most worries turn out to be about optimization, and
this module is the argument that optimization is not the relevant variable. Those
you can put down.

**What it means if nothing happens:** if the worry survives the two questions and
there is a genuine clinical question inside it, hearing, vision, feeding, tone,
responsiveness, that is exactly the kind of thing to raise with your
pediatrician. A specific question answered by a professional is a better use of
your energy than general dread.

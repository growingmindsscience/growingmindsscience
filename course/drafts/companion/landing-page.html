What this course is

It is a course you watch at your own pace, not a program. You are not being told how to parent. You are being told what the research on the first year actually establishes, what it only suggests, and where the popular version has run ahead of the findings.

Every module carries the same four parts: the lessons, five things you could try this week, an explicit list of the claims the module refuses to make, and the citations behind all of it with their replication status marked.

It will not make your baby sleep, develop faster, or turn out better. No course can do that, and the ones that say otherwise are selling something. What it can do is make the next twelve months more legible, so that fewer of them are spent guessing.

The part other courses leave out

Some of what you have been told does not hold up.

Every module ends with a section called "What this module is not saying." It names the popular claims in that territory and explains, with the citation, why the course will not repeat them. Three examples of what is in there.

  • You have probably heard

    "There is a thirty million word gap, so talk more."

    The Hart and Risley finding came from a very small sample, and Sperry, Sperry and Miller (2019) failed to replicate it. Nothing in this course asks you to count words. What the evidence points at is the quality and contingency of interaction, which is a different target and a kinder one.

  • You have probably heard

    "Babies are born knowing right from wrong."

    That claim rests on the helper and hinderer studies. Lucca et al. (2025), a ManyBabies replication running 1,018 infants across 37 labs, did not find the effect. Notably, the author of the original finding is a co-author on the replication. A null result is not proof of the opposite, so the course says the effect did not replicate and stops there.

  • You have probably heard

    "Crying peaks at six weeks, then improves."

    The decline is real and well supported. The peak is not. Wolke, Bilgin and Samara (2017) pooled diary data from 8,690 infants and reported no statistical evidence for a universal peak. The durations across the first six weeks were not distinguishable from one another. So the course tells you the honest and still useful version: the early weeks are the loud ones, and it reliably eases by around three months.

This is the actual difference. Plenty of courses promise confidence. This one also tells you when the science is thinner than the headline, which is the only way the confidence is worth anything.

Honest answers

Will this help my baby sleep?

It will not, and any course that promises it should worry you. Module 5 has a full lesson on sleep that explains what the research supports, what it does not, and why the choice about sleep training is a values decision rather than a settled scientific one. That is information, not a result.

Is this going to tell me I am doing it wrong?

No. There is no "is your baby behind" framing anywhere in it. Several of the modules exist specifically to take pressure off: mismatch and repair are normal parts of interaction, missing cues is the ordinary condition of parenting, and how much your baby cries in the first months is not a report card on you.

Who is it for?

Parents and caregivers of babies from birth to twelve months. It works during pregnancy too, and it works if your baby is already nine months old, since the modules are ordered but not time-locked.

How long will it take?

As long as you want. Each module is a set of narrated slides, about an hour if you watch it straight through, with no schedule and no live sessions. Most people watch one module in a sitting or across several shorter ones.

Is this medical advice?

No. It is developmental education. The modules flag the specific situations that warrant a call to your pediatrician, and they route perinatal mental health concerns to real support rather than trying to handle them. Anything about your particular baby's health belongs with your clinician.

Where do the claims come from?

Peer-reviewed developmental research, cited at the end of every module, with contested and failed-replication findings marked as such. Where a finding is mixed, the course says it is mixed instead of picking the tidier side.

Start whenever you are ready

There is no window closing and no reason to hurry. The first year is long, and the course will be here for the whole of it.

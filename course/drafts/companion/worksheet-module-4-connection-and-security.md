# Worksheet · Module 4 · Connection and Security

**What this is for:** a place to write down what you actually notice while you
try the five activities from Module 4, so the observations stay yours instead of
becoming a score.

Nothing on this page measures your baby's attachment. Attachment security is a
research classification produced by trained coders watching a standardized
procedure, and it is not something a worksheet, a video, or a parent at home can
assess. What you can do here is watch ordinary moments closely and notice what
you are already doing.

---

## 1. The reunion, deliberately

**What to do:** Next time you have been apart from your baby for more than a few
minutes, whether that is a work day, a nap, or ten minutes in another room, slow
your return down. Get to their level, greet them, and let them come to you and
take as much contact as they want. Do not rush them back into play.

**Write it down**

Date: `[____________]`  How long apart: `[____________]`

What they did first when they saw me:
`________________________________________________`

Did contact change their state? (fussing winding down, body softening)
`________________________________________________`

Did they turn back toward the room and their toys afterward? Yes `[ ]` No `[ ]`
Not sure `[ ]`

**What you are looking for:** whether contact with you actually works as a source
of settling when your baby wants one.

**What it means if nothing happens:** some babies barely register short
separations, and some are mid-nap-transition and just want to be put down. A
baby who does not need much comforting at reunion is not showing you a problem.
You are looking for whether you work as a source of settling when they want one,
not for how much they want one.

---

## 2. Watch one mismatch and one repair

**What to do:** During any ordinary five minutes of face-to-face time, look for
one moment where you and your baby are out of sync. You lean in as they look
away. You offer the rattle and they wanted your face. Then watch the next fifteen
seconds or so as one of you adjusts.

**Write it down**

The mismatch (what went out of sync):
`________________________________________________`

Who adjusted first? Me `[ ]`  Baby `[ ]`  Both `[ ]`  Could not tell `[ ]`

The repair (what brought you back into step):
`________________________________________________`

**What you are looking for:** the correction. Notice that you are already doing
this constantly without naming it. That cycle is the mechanism from Lesson 4.2.

**What it means if nothing happens:** if you cannot spot a clean example, that is
a limitation of watching in real time, not evidence that your interaction is
unusually smooth or unusually rough. Micro-coding this takes trained observers
and slow video. Try again another day, or let it go.

---

## 3. Name the goodbye

**What to do:** Pick one short phrase and one small gesture and use exactly the
same ones every time you leave, even for five minutes in another room. Say it, do
it, go. Do not extend it, and do not sneak out.

**Write it down**

My phrase: `________________________________________________`

My gesture: `________________________________________________`

Days used this week: `[ ] [ ] [ ] [ ] [ ] [ ] [ ]`

Any sign of anticipation when I say it:
`________________________________________________`

Did the protest get shorter, longer, or stay the same?
`________________________________________________`

How it changed me (a script removes some of the deciding):
`________________________________________________`

**What you are looking for:** over a couple of weeks, whether your baby begins to
show any anticipation of the phrase, and whether the protest shortens.

**What it means if nothing happens:** under about six months, most babies will
not respond to the routine at all, because the underlying memory and
person-permanence abilities are still developing. Later, plenty of babies protest
just as loudly with a perfect routine as without one. The routine is for
predictability, not for preventing distress.

## 4. One two-minute state check on yourself

**What to do:** Next time your baby is crying and you feel your own system
winding up, take two minutes before or during the soothing to deliberately slow
your breathing, drop your shoulders, and notice your voice pitch. If it is safe
to do so and you are past your limit, put your baby down somewhere safe and step
away for a moment first.

**Write it down**

My state before (1 = steady, 5 = winding up): `[1] [2] [3] [4] [5]`

What I changed (breathing, shoulders, voice, stepped away):
`________________________________________________`

My state after: `[1] [2] [3] [4] [5]`

Did my baby's state follow mine at all, on any lag?
`________________________________________________`

**What you are looking for:** whether your baby's state follows yours at all, on
any lag. Sometimes it is quick. Often it is not.

**What it means if nothing happens:** plenty of crying has causes that no amount
of your calm will resolve, including pain, hunger, illness, and long unexplained
evening crying. Your steadiness is not a switch, and failing to soothe a crying
baby is not a failure of co-regulation. Being calm while unable to fix it still
counts.

## 5. Audit one source of attachment advice

**What to do:** Take one piece of attachment content you have seen recently, from
anywhere, and check it against Module 4.

**Write it down**

Does it claim a specific practice (carrying, bedsharing, feeding method) produces
secure attachment? Yes `[ ]` No `[ ]`

`________________________________________________`

**What you are looking for:** the gap between "attachment security" as a measured
relationship construct and "attachment parenting" as a commercial set of
practices. They share a word and very little else.

**What it means if nothing happens:** if the content holds up, good, keep the
source. This is not an exercise in finding fault. It is an exercise in noticing
which claims come with a study behind them.

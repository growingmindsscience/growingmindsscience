The classes

Who they’re for, what’s open, and what each one covers.

Who are these classes for?

Parents of children roughly 0–5, plus the adults who care for and support them: partners, co-parents, grandparents, and caregivers who want to understand what’s developmentally happening underneath behavior. The toddler class is built for parents of children between 12 and 36 months; the infant class is for parents of babies in the first year.

Which classes are available?

Three classes are open now: Birth to 12 months, the toddler years (ages 1–3), and the preschool years (ages 3–5). Each is $49 with lifetime access, or you can enroll in all three classes together for $119, which saves $28. The toddler class and the all-three enrollment include Ask the research, the AI tool, with a fair-use limit of 50 questions a day.

The family-systems class is still in development. Join the waitlist on its page and you’ll get one email when it opens, plus an occasional research note, with no launch-date countdowns or urgency tactics.

What does each class cover?

Each class has four modules:

  • Birth to 12 months: The Newborn Brain; Reading Your Baby’s Cues; Attachment in the First Year; The Explorer: Language, Movement, and Play.
  • Toddler years: The Toddler Brain, Briefly; Language, the Everyday Version; Autonomy and No; Big Feelings, Transitions, and Repair.
  • Preschool years: The Thinking Preschooler; Play as the Work; Friends and Feelings; Ready for School.

The toddler class, for example, is built around the developmental window from 12 to 36 months: what’s happening in your child’s brain, body, and emotional life, and why it shows up as the behaviors you see every day. Topics include language development, the emergence of autonomy and identity, emotion regulation (and why co-regulation comes first), tantrums, sleep, limits, and the transition out of toddlerhood.

Are they self-paced?

Yes. Every class is fully self-paced and asynchronous, with no live sessions to attend. Lessons are short and designed to be watched in 10–20 minute pieces, during a nap or after bedtime. You can return to anything as often as you like.

What format are the classes: video, text, or both?

Primarily short video lessons, designed to be watched in 10–20 minute pieces. Each lesson is paired with written summaries and practical guides so you can revisit the key ideas without re-watching if you prefer. The format is built around real parenting schedules: nap time, after bedtime, or a quiet 20 minutes whenever you have one.

Buying and access

Where the classes live, refunds, and what stays free. Every price is on the pricing page.

Where are classes hosted and how do I access them?

All three classes run inside your From Little Onward account under My classes: enroll, pay with Stripe, and the lessons open right away on any device. All three are self-paced with lifetime access.

You can get a full refund within 14 days of buying, as long as you’ve watched 4 lessons or fewer (the free lesson doesn’t count): email Matthew with the address you bought with. To see the teaching before you buy, watch a full infant lesson free.

What kinds of tools and resources are available?

The free tools live on the Tools for Parents page. The milestone tracker is live and available now, alongside behavior, sleep, screen-time, and regulation guides: short, plain-language references organized by topic, not a blog archive. The Articles page is for longer pieces that translate research and patterns from coaching.

The approach

Where the science comes from, who it’s for, and what it isn’t.

Are they evidence-informed?

Yes. The classes draw on peer-reviewed developmental science across cognitive development, language development, environmental influences on skill development, emotion regulation, and parenting behaviors. Where the research is strong, that’s reflected. Where it’s mixed or uncertain, that’s said honestly, instead of being flattened into false certainty.

Are they only for parents?

The primary audience is parents, but the work is useful to anyone who wants developmental science explained clearly: partners, co-parents, grandparents, caregivers, and professionals who work with families. The tone assumes you’re already paying attention and capable of nuance.

Is this medical or psychological advice?

No. From Little Onward is parent education only. It is not a substitute for medical, psychological, or developmental advice from your child’s clinicians. If you have a specific concern about your child, the right next step is a conversation with their pediatrician or a developmental specialist.

Who built this?

Matthew McArthur, a Child Development Specialist who studied early language and cognition at San Diego State University, wrote “Why Early Chatter Matters” for Frontiers for Young Minds, and spent two years in state-funded early intervention, coaching more than 100 families of children under three.
